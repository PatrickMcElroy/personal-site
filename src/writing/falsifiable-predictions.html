In “The Policy World is Waking Up,” I compared the act of policymaking in AI to the creation of benchmarks; they allow you the leverage to shape AI development without the kind of capital you would otherwise need. For both, however, you need the same ability in order to reliably convert your efforts into any directional effect — you have to be able to intuit the shape of AI progress, what models will remain bad at, and what they might surprisingly become capable of. If your benchmark is saturated too soon or the models can reward hack around it, it becomes almost useless. If a policy doesn’t address the risks that will be around once it passes or is easily sidestepped by the firms or labs it regulates, it will also be ineffective. Accurate forecasting is necessary and too often neglected, especially in the policy space.

With that in mind, I’m hoping to accompany each of my posts with a tractable, falsifiable prediction about the world. This will help clarify the opinion of the post and grade its accuracy in the future. To start, I’ll offer a few predictions from my initial posts.

From “The Policy World is Waking Up,” I predict that lobbying spend in the AI sector will eclipse all other industries by 2029. Specifically, I think that spending in 2027 and 2028 combined will be over $1 billion (after only 100 million in 2025) and that OpenAI and Anthropic will each spend or donate over $100 million (directly or via proxy donations) over those two years.

From “The Inverted Surveillance State,” I predict that we will see federal Congress or more than two federal agencies turn off or overhaul access to previously public data by the end of 2028. For example, this would include rate-limiting the creation of FOIA requests or tying them to an identity requirement as a US citizen in response to AI driven request volume.

From “The Individualized Political Campaign,” I predict that we will see a campaign or PAC before the end of 2028 spend over $50 million on custom advertising that is AI-generated based on user data. This would include social media ad variation based on user data, generative TV or YouTube spot ads, or some kind of conversational persuasive chatbot interface.
