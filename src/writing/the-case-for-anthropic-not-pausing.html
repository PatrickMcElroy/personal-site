I’ve seen a lot of commentary recently condemning Anthropic for not joining OpenAI in announcing a pause on some frontier training. I haven’t really seen the case made for their not pausing being consistent with prioritizing safety, so I hope to lay one out quickly here.

From Anthropic’s perspective, the HuggingFace incident generally lends credence to the following narratives:

  • OpenAI, specifically Sam, has a track record of acting differently than previously signaled

  • Novel and dangerous emergent capabilities, specifically as a result of swarm behavior, are likely not far away

  • OpenAI’s internal safety infrastructure and monitoring is less well resourced than their own

In a multi-agent iterated game with a current state of total defection, cooperation is trusted when it can be concretely verified or else believed based on the other agent’s historical actions and incentives. For the most powerful agent with a preference for total cooperation, signaling cooperation with another powerful agent only makes sense if the payoff outweighs the expected cost of defection from the other agent (cost * likelihood). It seems like the HuggingFace incident would meaningfully increase both the cost of OpenAI defecting and its likelihood.

Given the same level of signaled slowdown and OpenAI’s track record, Anthropic would expect OpenAI to perform less meaningful slowdown. This would result in dangerous capabilities being more likely to emerge inside of OpenAI first. Given the demonstrated weakness of their infrastructure and monitoring, this would also raise the chance of real-world catastrophic damage or even outright takeover. In Anthropic’s view, reaching dangerous capabilities in their own training allows them to push for AI for AI safety and gives them more leverage to change the structure of the multi-agent dynamic to incentivize verified cooperation.

As an outside observer, I recognize this argument is a bit of the classic AI-safetyism meme. Personally, I do trust Anthropic more than OpenAI to use their power and leverage in a way that is more consistent with long-run safety. However, we desperately need enforced third party observability — we should not have to rely on trust! I worry that an Anthropic announcement of some semi-verifiable pause would actually work against the pressure toward third-party monitoring of these labs. These organizations cannot (yet) speak in neuralese, and this is a crucial moment to ensure that their human chains of thought are not hacking toward the massive commercial rewards in front of them. Organizations like METR and Redwood Research should not be limited to occasional internal audits at the labs’ discretion, and I urge Anthropic to advocate for empowering them (or other similar institutions) at the highest level possible.
